# Dossier: Flawless Consulting (Peter Block)
**Key:** `fc` | **Target Practice:** Forward Deployed Engineering & Deployment Strategy

---

## 1. Core Thesis in 5 Lines
1. Consulting without formal direct line authority requires authentic dialogue, clear contracting, and collaborative partnership.
2. Every business problem has two simultaneous dimensions: the technical/business problem and the relationship/management problem.
3. Client resistance is a predictable, natural emotional reaction to vulnerability and loss of control; it is not a rational debate.
4. Answering emotional resistance with more data or analytical logic intensifies the pushback and damages the relationship.
5. The only effective response to resistance is to name it neutrally, pause in silence, and share responsibility 50/50.

---

## 2. Chapter-by-Chapter Map (4th Edition)

* **Ch 1: A Consultant by Any Other Name:** Defines the consultant role: anyone trying to have influence with no direct line authority. Introduces the Expert, Pair-of-Hands, and Collaborative models.
* **Ch 2: Techniques Are Not Enough:** Explains that personal authenticity and emotional presence matter more than analytical tools.
* **Ch 3: Flawless Consulting:** Developing client commitment, reducing bad data, and operating under a 50/50 responsibility covenant.
* **Ch 4: Contracting Overview:** The foundation of success. The contracting conversation establishes mutual wants, boundaries, and client investment.
* **Ch 5: The Contracting Meeting:** Detailed mechanics: Agendas, expressing wants, handling mixed signals, agreeing on deliverables.
* **Ch 6: The Agonies of Contracting:** What to do when a client says "just give me a proposal" or refuses to invest their own team's time.
* **Ch 7: The Internal Consultant:** Specific dynamics for in-house and embedded deployers who cannot walk away from the client company.
* **Ch 8: Understanding Resistance:** The psychology of fear, losing control, and exposure of organizational failure.
* **Ch 9: Recognizing Resistance:** Catalog of resistance forms: The detail trap, flooding, time excuses, attacks, confusion, instant compliance.
* **Ch 10: Dealing with Resistance:** The 3-step sequence: Identify the form, name it neutrally without punishment, remain silent and let the client respond.
* **Ch 11: From Diagnosis to Discovery:** Shifting from treating the client as a patient to engaging in joint diagnosis.
* **Ch 12: The Discovery Interview:** Asking about the human and political system around the technical challenge.
* **Ch 13: Preparing for Feedback:** Condensing observations into clear, non-jargon language before the feedback meeting.
* **Ch 14: Managing the Meeting for Action:** Structuring the feedback meeting: Present findings simply, explore client reactions, manage resistance, confirm decision to act.
* **Ch 15: Implementation:** Moving from decision into action without falling back into the Pair-of-Hands role.

## 3. Step-by-Step Toolkits for a Deployed Strategist

### A. The 9 Forms of Client Resistance & Naming Scripts

1. **The Endless Detail Trap:** Client demands more data/breakdowns.
   * *Script:* *"I notice that every time we get close to deciding on the pilot rollout, we pivot to requesting another layer of baseline data. Are you concerned that the current evidence isn't defensible to your leadership?"*
2. **Flooding with Detail:** Client sends 500 unorganized files.
   * *Script:* *"This is a massive volume of material. My sense is that wading through all of this right now might delay addressing the core operational block on Line 1. Where should we focus first?"*
3. **Time / Impracticality:** *"We're too swamped right now."*
   * *Script:* *"You’re telling me timing is impossible, which tells me this project feels like an added burden rather than a relief. What would have to shift for this to feel urgent?"*
4. **Attack / Questioning Credibility:** *"How do you outsiders know our plant?"*
   * *Script:* *"You’re questioning our methodology and experience. It feels like there’s anxiety about whether external models will reflect your operators' daily reality. Let’s talk about that."*
5. **Sudden Confusion:** *"I'm just not following what you mean."*
   * *Script:* *"We walked through this architecture yesterday and it was clear. Something changed overnight. What new doubt came up?"*
6. **Instant Passive Compliance:** *"Sure, sure, whatever you say."*
   * *Script:* *"You’re agreeing very quickly without pushing back on the shift requirements. I’m worried you’re saying yes just to check the box, but won't hold the team to it."*
7. **Intellectualizing:** Debating theoretical models.
   * *Script:* *"We are debating optimization algorithms, but avoiding what happens to shift supervisors when this goes live. Can we look at the human impact?"*
8. **Blaming Others:** *"It's IT's fault."*
   * *Script:* *"IT isn't in the room right now. What is in our direct power to control within your operational unit?"*
9. **Problem Disappearance:** *"It's actually not that bad."*
   * *Script:* *"Three weeks ago this downtime was costing \$80k/day. What makes it feel manageable now?"*

### B. The 50/50 Contracting Conversation Structure
1. State the presenting problem in plain language.
2. Outline what you (the Strategist) want from the client: access, dedicated engineering hours, weekly decision reviews.
3. Ask the client what they want from you: deliverables, timelines, confidentiality.
4. Agree on roles: Explicitly reject the Pair-of-Hands model (*"We build it together; if our team writes code in isolation, your engineers will reject it when we leave"*).
5. Establish the feedback loop: How conflicts will be handled directly.

## 5. Field Playbook for Deployed Strategists

| Trigger in Deployment | FDE Action | Artifact / Script |
|---|---|---|
| **Plant Director treats FDE as a contractor code-monkey.** | Recontract explicitly to Collaborative model. | *"If we write this without your process engineers alongside us, it will die on handoff. We need a 50/50 partnership."* |
| **Middle management resists using new AI scheduling UI.** | Diagnose resistance as fear of obsolescence; name it. | *"This isn't about UI button placement; it feels like supervisors are worried this replaces their judgment."* |
| **Feedback meeting gets derailed into methodology critique.** | Stop defending the math; name the emotional reaction. | Be silent for 10 seconds. Let the client sit with the finding. Ask: *"What is hardest to hear about this finding?"* |
| **Client demands custom features outside pilot scope.** | Refer back to original Contracting Document. | Review mutual wants/offers signed at kickoff; negotiate trade-offs openly. |
