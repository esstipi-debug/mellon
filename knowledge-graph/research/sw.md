# Dossier: Switch: How to Change Things When Change Is Hard (Chip & Dan Heath)
**Key:** `sw` | **Target Practice:** Forward Deployed Engineering & Deployment Strategy

---

## 1. Core Thesis in 5 Lines
1. Organizational change fails because leaders address only the rational mind while ignoring emotional motivation and environmental friction.
2. The Rider (rational mind) needs crystal-clear direction: find the bright spots, script the critical moves, and point to a vivid destination.
3. The Elephant (emotional mind) needs feeling: shrink the change, create early identity wins, and turn abstract metrics into visceral experiences.
4. The Path (environment) must be shaped: eliminate physical/digital friction, tweak defaults, build habit triggers, and rally social proof.
5. What looks like human resistance is almost always a lack of behavioral clarity; what looks like laziness is exhaustion of self-control.

---

## 2. Chapter-by-Chapter Map

* **Ch 1: Three Surprises About Change:** The Rider, Elephant, and Path metaphor. Explains why willpower fails and why change requires harmony across all three.
* **Ch 2: Find the Bright Spots (Direct the Rider):** Investigate where the problem is *already* being solved within the organization and clone that success (e.g., Jerry Sternin's malnutrition study in Vietnam).
* **Ch 3: Script the Critical Moves (Direct the Rider):** Eliminate ambiguity. Don't say "eat healthier"; say "switch from whole milk to 1% milk". Specific behaviors prevent decision paralysis.
* **Ch 4: Point to the Destination (Direct the Rider):** Craft a compelling "Destination Postcard" and black-and-white goals so the team knows exactly what victory looks like.
* **Ch 5: Find the Feeling (Motivate the Elephant):** Knowing is not enough; people must feel the problem. (e.g., Dumping 424 different work gloves on the executive boardroom table to illustrate procurement waste).
* **Ch 6: Shrink the Change (Motivate the Elephant):** Break the initial step down until it feels trivial. Leverage the endowed progress effect (e.g., Car wash loyalty cards with 2 free pre-stamped slots get completed far faster).
* **Ch 7: Grow Your People (Motivate the Elephant):** Foster an identity mindset: *"We are the kind of plant that leads in precision engineering."* Frame failures as standard learning curves.
* **Ch 8: Tweak the Environment (Shape the Path):** Change the default layout, software settings, or workstation tools to make the desired behavior the path of least resistance.
* **Ch 9: Build Habits (Shape the Path):** Utilize action triggers (*"When event X happens, I will execute action Y"*) and checklists to reduce cognitive drain on the Rider.
* **Ch 10: Rally the Herd (Shape the Path):** Make positive behavior publicly visible. People look to peers for behavioral cues in unfamiliar environments.
* **Ch 11: Keep the Switch Going:** Celebrating early milestones, reinforcing momentum, and institutionalizing new norms.

## 3. Step-by-Step Toolkits for a Deployed Strategist

### A. The Rider-Elephant-Path Deployment Canvas
Before rolling out new operational software across a plant or business unit:
1. **Direct the Rider:**
   - *Bright Spot:* Which single operator or station is already using the tool effectively? What are they doing differently?
   - *Scripted Moves:* What are the exact 3 clicks operators must do at the start of a shift? (Write a 1-page visual cheat sheet).
   - *Destination:* *"Zero backorders by end of month."*
2. **Motivate the Elephant:**
   - *Find the Feeling:* Show operators a video or physical pile of scrapped parts caused by current manual miscommunication.
   - *Shrink the Change:* First week goal: Log in once and confirm 1 workorder (takes 30 seconds).
   - *Identity:* *"You are the master process specialists testing this to make it foolproof for junior operators."*
3. **Shape the Path:**
   - *Tweak the Environment:* Set the new tool as the automatic default browser homepage on all terminal screens; bookmark legacy software out of sight.
   - *Build Habits:* Add an Action Trigger: *"When the shift bell rings at 7:00 AM, review the Top 3 automated alerts."*
   - *Rally the Herd:* Post a physical board showing daily adoption percentages by shift team.

## 5. Field Playbook for Deployed Strategists

| Trigger in Deployment | FDE Action | Artifact / Script |
|---|---|---|
| **Operators complain the new system is 'too confusing'.** | Script the Critical Moves; eliminate decision paralysis. | Strip UI complexity; publish a 3-step laminated action card attached to terminal monitors. |
| **Middle management resists giving up old Excel sheets.** | Find the Bright Spots. | Interview the 1 supervisor who loves the new system; have them present their workflow to peers. |
| **Low engagement during pilot week.** | Shrink the Change. | Reduce pilot scope: *"We only ask you to use the tool for the first 15 minutes of Tuesday morning."* |
| **New tool adoption fades after FDE leaves site.** | Tweak Environment & Build Action Triggers. | Lock default login screen to the application; automate calendar invites for daily review triggers. |
