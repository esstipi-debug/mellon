# Dossier: The Trusted Advisor (David H. Maister, Charles H. Green, Robert M. Galford)
**Key:** `ta` | **Target Practice:** Forward Deployed Engineering & Deployment Strategy

---

## 1. Core Thesis in 5 Lines
1. Professional technical competence and intellectual brilliance alone do not create high-impact advisory relationships; trust is the prerequisite.
2. The Trust Equation formalizes trustworthiness: $(C + R + I) / S$, where Self-Orientation ($S$) is the denominator and greatest destroyer of trust.
3. Trust is built through a disciplined 5-step conversational sequence: Engage $\rightarrow$ Listen $\rightarrow$ Frame $\rightarrow$ Envision $\rightarrow$ Commit.
4. Advisors must earn the right to propose solutions by demonstrating deep, empathetic listening to both rational and emotional realities.
5. In client interactions, advisors must let the client own the final decision while boldly naming elephant-in-the-room risks ("Name it and claim it").

## 2. Chapter-by-Chapter Map

* **Ch 1: A First View of Trust:** Trust is an emotional bond, not a contractual SLA. Why technical expertise without trust is commoditized.
* **Ch 2: The Development of Trust:** How relationships mature from vendor $\rightarrow$ problem solver $\rightarrow$ trusted strategic advisor.
* **Ch 3: The Trust Equation:** Analytical decomposition of Credibility, Reliability, Intimacy, and Self-Orientation.
* **Ch 4: The Dynamics of Trust:** The asymmetric nature of trust: hard to build, fragile to keep, fast to destroy.
* **Ch 5: The Five Steps to Trusting:** The conversational process: Engage, Listen, Frame, Envision, Commit.
* **Ch 6: Step One: Engage:** Getting attention on issues that matter to the client, leading with curiosity rather than pitching.
* **Ch 7: Step Two: Listen:** Active and empathetic listening; understanding emotional undertones; asking open questions.
* **Ch 8: Step Three: Frame:** Clarifying the root issue; reframing presenting complaints into strategic choices.
* **Ch 9: Step Four: Envision:** Co-creating the vision of the future; asking *"What would success look like to you?"*
* **Ch 10: Step Five: Commit:** Agreeing on mutual actions and ensuring the client has skin in the game.
* **Ch 11: What’s So Hard About All This?:** Dealing with advisor ego, the fear of not knowing the answer, and excessive self-orientation.
* **Ch 12: Differing Trust Personalities:** Adapting to diverse client working styles and trust temperaments.
* **Ch 13: The Role of Trust in Getting Hired:** Trust-based selling: focus on helping the client solve their problem during the sales meeting rather than pitching credentials.
* **Ch 14: Overcoming Resistance & Building Capital:** Naming awkward topics with diplomacy and courage.
* **Ch 15: The Trusted Advisor Fieldbook:** Implementation protocols for consulting practices.

## 3. Step-by-Step Toolkits for a Deployed Strategist

### A. The Trust Equation Diagnostic & Mitigation Checklist

$$\text{Trustworthiness} = \frac{\text{Credibility} + \text{Reliability} + \text{Intimacy}}{\text{Self-Orientation}}$$

* **Credibility ($C$ - Head):**
  * *Audit:* Do I admit what I don't know? Do I provide calibrated ranges instead of false certainty?
  * *Fix:* Say: *"I haven't analyzed your specific database schema yet, so I cannot guarantee latency. Let's run a test tomorrow to find out."*
* **Reliability ($R$ - Hand):**
  * *Audit:* Do I send meeting notes within 2 hours? Do I hit promised deployment milestones?
  * *Fix:* If a deliverable will be 30 minutes late, notify the client before the deadline, not after.
* **Intimacy ($I$ - Heart):**
  * *Audit:* Does the client feel safe discussing political friction, team incompetence, or fear of failure?
  * *Fix:* Protect client confidentiality rigorously; validate emotional stress without judgment.
* **Self-Orientation ($S$ - Denominator):**
  * *Audit:* Am I trying to sound smart? Am I pushing a technology stack to pad my resume or company quota?
  * *Fix:* Focus 100% on the client’s career safety and operational stability.

### B. "Name It and Claim It" Protocol
When tension or risk is obvious in a client deployment meeting:
1. **Name the Dynamic:** Put the uncomfortable issue into words.
2. **Claim Your Part:** Take personal ownership so the client doesn't feel attacked.
3. *Script:* *"This is awkward to bring up, but I noticed our weekly standup attendance dropped by half after last week's outage. I'm worried our team pushed changes too fast and damaged your confidence in the system. Can we talk about how that felt on your end?"*

## 5. Field Playbook for Deployed Strategists

| Trigger in Deployment | FDE Action | Artifact / Script |
|---|---|---|
| **Client asks a difficult technical question you don't know.** | Protect Credibility: admit uncertainty openly; provide a timeframe. | *"I don't know the answer to that edge-case yet, and I won't guess. I'll test it in sandbox and report back by 4 PM."* |
| **Client director seems detached and defensive.** | Lower Self-Orientation; execute Active Listening. | Stop demonstrating software features. Ask: *"What is the most stressful part of this deployment for your group right now?"* |
| **Client is reluctant to sign off on production rollout.** | Co-create the Commit step; let client own decision. | *"Here are the 2 deployment paths with trade-offs. You know your operators best: which approach do you want to take?"* |
| **Stakeholder is badmouthing the project in hallway chatter.** | Use "Name It and Claim It" in a 1-on-1 coffee chat. | *"I sensed you were uncomfortable with the new automated alerting. I wanted to check in directly to understand your concerns."* |
